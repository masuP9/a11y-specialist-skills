# 1.4.9 Images of Text (No Exception) (AAA)

- Scope: When evaluating AAA, images that contain text
- Materials: The same as [1.4.5](./1.4.5.md) (the image content, its name, caption, and surrounding text), plus whether a switch to a text presentation exists
- Automated check: None
- Sources checked: 2026-11-11. WCAG: Recommendation of 2024-12-12. Understanding updated 2025-09-16; C22 updated 2026-01-12; C30 updated 2026-03-09 (living public pages). The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 1.4.9-E01 — Allowed only for decoration and where essential

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Images of Text (No Exception)](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#images-of-text-no-exception), [Term — pure decoration](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-pure-decoration), [Term — essential](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-essential), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text-no-exception.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Images of text may be used only for pure decoration or where a particular presentation of text is essential to the information conveyed. Logotypes are considered essential. Essential means information would be lost without that presentation (text that shows a particular font family, or text that conveys identity such as a company logo).
- **Conditions / limits:** The sources give examples of what is essential but no exhaustive list.

### 1.4.9-E02 — Images that are not images of text

- **Type / source:** [Term — images of text](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-images-of-text), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text-no-exception.html#intent), [Understanding (1.4.6) — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Text in a picture that contains significant other visual content (graphs, screenshots, diagrams) is not an image of text. The 1.4.6 Understanding says this exclusion is meant to separate pictures that have text in them from images of text made to replace text in order to get a particular look.
- **Conditions / limits:** The sources give no example of which side an image with text laid over a photo falls on.

### 1.4.9-E03 — Differences from 1.4.5

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Images of Text (No Exception)](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#images-of-text-no-exception), [Normative — Images of Text](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#images-of-text), [Understanding (1.4.5) — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html#intent).
- **Summary:** 1.4.9 lacks 1.4.5's condition "if the technologies being used can achieve the visual presentation" and its exception for images that can be visually customized to the user's requirements. Instead it has an exception for pure decoration. The note in the 1.4.5 Understanding that providing the same information as adjustable text satisfies the criterion does not appear in the 1.4.9 Understanding.
- **Conditions / limits:** "Essential" and the note in the images-of-text definition use the same wording as in 1.4.5.

### 1.4.9-E04 — Switching to a text presentation

- **Type / source:** [Technique — C30 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C30#description), [C22 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C22#description) (both Sufficient).
- **Summary:** C30 provides a presentation that replaces HTML text with images of text and one that keeps the text, plus a control that lets users switch between them; it says this can meet 1.4.9 if a presentation without images of text is available. C22 uses CSS to create the visual presentation of text.
- **Conditions / limits:** Confirm that the same information and functionality are available after switching. Techniques are not the only way.

### 1.4.9-E05 — Examples in Understanding

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — Examples](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text-no-exception.html#examples).
- **Summary:** Examples that meet it: a quote and navigation styled with CSS, a company logo image, a representation of a font family, an image of an original letter that conveys the time period, and symbolic characters on text-formatting buttons (`B` for bold, `I` for italic, `ABC` for spell check). The symbolic characters do not form a sequence expressing something in human language.
- **Conditions / limits:** The examples are not exhaustive requirements.

## Judgment lines

- An image of text that conveys the content of the text, is neither decorative nor essential, and has no switch to a text presentation is an AAA failure. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E01, E04]
- Use the 1.4.5 judgment lines that rest on the images-of-text definition and the reading of "essential" as they are. Graphs, diagrams, screenshots of apps or sites, and illustrations of screens are out of scope; so are street signs that happen to appear in photos and tick labels in charts. Images showing only a company or brand name are treated as logos and are out of scope. Font samples, typesetting examples, handwriting, and visual comparisons are out of scope. Covers of documents and books, and thumbnails of events and articles, are out of scope. Not using the alt content as a clue, and the treatment when the image cannot be viewed, are also the same as in 1.4.5. [1.4.5's judgment lines](./1.4.5.md#judgment-lines) are the source of truth; do not copy them here. [Author policy: the definition and "essential" use the same wording, so we carry over 1.4.5's reading (including its `[Author policy]` lines). Changing the reading only for 1.4.9 would contradict 1.4.5. Evidence: 1.4.9-E01–E03]
- Do not pass an image only because the same information is also given as visible text. Alt text and hidden text for screen readers do not substitute either. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E03. Existing policy of not extending 1.4.5's visible-text route to 1.4.9]
- A mechanism that lets users choose the color or size of text in an image (such as an image regenerated from user settings) is not an exception under 1.4.9. It passes if users can switch to a text presentation with the same content. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E03, E04]
- Main visuals and banners with a catchphrase or heading baked into a photo or illustration are in scope for 1.4.9 and are AAA failures. Do not keep this kind of image out of scope for 1.4.9 just because 1.4.5 does. However, images where the lettering design itself is the artwork (hand-lettered titles, an event name designed as a logo) are essential and out of scope, and decorative text whose words could be substituted without changing its purpose is also out of scope. [Author policy: 1.4.5 kept these out of scope because "making only the text HTML cannot reproduce the same look," a condition that exists only in 1.4.5. We read a baked-in catchphrase as an image of text made to replace text for a particular look. Evidence: 1.4.9-E01–E03]
- Symbolic characters that do not express human language (`B` and `I` on text-formatting buttons, `ABC` for spell check) are not treated as images of text. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E05]
- Evaluate 1.4.9 only in audits whose target level includes AAA, or when asked to look at AAA. When you evaluate it, report it as AAA in a table separate from AA, and do not count it in AA Pass/Fail totals. In AA audits and reviews, do not list images that fall only under 1.4.9 (images that are out of scope or pass under 1.4.5) even as improvement candidates. AA is judged under 1.4.5. [Author policy: reporting scope that keeps AAA results out of AA failures. Evidence: 1.4.9-E01, E03]

## Judgment examples

- **AAA failure (passes 1.4.5):** An announcement is an image on a plain background, and the same sentences are also placed next to it as visible text. The visible text meets 1.4.5 but not 1.4.9. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E03]
- **AAA failure (out of scope for 1.4.5):** The top-page main visual with a catchphrase baked into a photo. [Author policy: the banner treatment. Evidence: 1.4.9-E02, E03]
- **Pass:** A heading image can be switched, through an available control, to HTML text with the same content. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E04]
- **Out of scope:** The header logo image, images showing only client company names, font samples, document covers. [Author policy: carried over from 1.4.5's default policies. Evidence: 1.4.9-E01, E05]
- **Out of scope:** A film title image designed with hand-drawn lettering. [Author policy: when the lettering design is the artwork. Evidence: 1.4.9-E01]
- **Out of scope:** An editor's bold button draws `B` as an image. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E05]
- **Needs review:** The image cannot be retrieved, and neither its name nor the surrounding text shows whether it is a banner or a document cover. Retrieve the image itself and the surrounding presentation. [Author policy: inspection example. Evidence: 1.4.9-E01, E02]

## When building

- When aiming for AAA, make the main visual's catchphrase HTML text too and overlay it on the photo with CSS. Adding visible text alongside is not enough. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E03, E04]
- If you keep the image, provide a control that switches to a text presentation with the same content. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E04]

## When checking

- **What automation settles:** Nothing. Results of manually matching the image, its purpose, and any switch can be final. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E01–E04]
- **Remaining:** For each image containing text, sort it by 1.4.5's axis (the content of the text, or the look), then check decoration, essential presentation, and a switch to text. For images that passed 1.4.5 through visible text or visual customization, check again whether a switch exists. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E01–E04]
- **Do not assert:** Passing 1.4.5 does not mean 1.4.9 is met. Do not decide that something is an image of text without seeing the image. [Evidence: 1.4.9-E03]
