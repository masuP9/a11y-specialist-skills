# 1.2.1 Audio-only and Video-only (Prerecorded)

- Scope: Prerecorded audio-only and video-only presentations. Classify by actual audio, video, and time-based interaction, not element names or extensions
- Materials: Complete media, alternative text/audio, relationship to source text, and access to the alternative
- Automated check: Use DOM and links to collect candidates. Play the content to check equivalence
- Sources checked: 2026-11-02. WCAG: Recommendation of 2024-12-12; Understanding / Techniques: living published pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 1.2.1-E01 — Distinguish the routes for audio-only and video-only

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Audio-only and Video-only (Prerecorded)](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#audio-only-and-video-only-prerecorded), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-only-and-video-only-prerecorded.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Audio-only needs an equivalent alternative for time-based media; video-only can use that alternative or equivalent audio. Media clearly labeled as an alternative to text, adding no information, is excepted.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not universally require another transcript or captions for audio provided as an equivalent alternative to silent video. Distinguish synchronized audio/video from this criterion and check 1.2.2, 1.2.3, and 1.2.5. Do not assume media with time-based interaction is simply audio-only or video-only either.

### 1.2.1-E02 — Compare the audio alternative with the final recording

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G158 “Description / Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G158#description) (Sufficient).
- **Summary:** Represent dialogue, speakers, and informative sounds in the language of the audio. Correct a production script to match the final recording and provide access to the corresponding alternative.
- **Conditions / limits:** A title or summary alone is not equivalent to the entire audio. Check speaker attribution when there are multiple speakers. G158's example placement is not the only implementation.

### 1.2.1-E03 — Silent video can use either text or audio

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G159 “Description / Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G159#description), [G166 “Description / Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G166#description) (both Sufficient).
- **Summary:** Provide equivalent information about steps, actions, and on-screen text in sequenced text or audio. Audio may be a separate file reached through an identifiable link.
- **Conditions / limits:** Adding background music does not provide equivalent information. Check equivalence with the final video and access to the alternative. Do not require both text and audio.

## Judgment lines

- Assess an audio-only podcast under E02 and a silent instructional video under E03. Muting an audiovisual video does not make it video-only. If classification is unclear, obtain the audio track and playback state. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01–E03. Checking beyond attributes is our inspection policy]
- Do not flag narration/video clearly labeled as an alternative to source text when it adds no information. Nearby related articles or summaries alone do not establish the exception. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01]
- Pass when the corresponding alternative is usable and equivalent. Fail when an applicable presentation has no alternative or confirmed missing information. If only a link label is available, leave its destination content and comparison with the media for review. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E02–E03]
- Check descriptive identification such as a media title under [1.1.1](./1.1.1.md); a title alone does not satisfy this criterion's alternative requirement. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E02–E03, 1.1.1-E06]

## Judgment examples

- **Pass:** A recorded conversation has a final transcript including speakers, dialogue, and necessary sounds, available through its corresponding link. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E02]
- **Pass:** A silent assembly video links to an audio file equivalently explaining each action in order. Do not additionally require text. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01, E03]
- **Exception; do not flag:** A recording labeled as the audio version of the body text reads only that information. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01]
- **Fail:** An audio interview has only a three-line introduction, without the actual answers or another equivalent alternative. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E02]
- **Needs review:** A “Video description” link is inaccessible. Obtain the silent video and full description or audio to check content and access. [Our interpretation: inspection example. Evidence: 1.2.1-E03]

## When building

- Keep audio transcripts and silent-video descriptions aligned with the final media. Identify the media and make its corresponding alternative reachable. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E02–E03]
- Choose text or audio for silent video. When producing another presentation with synchronized audio and video, check the other requirements in 1.2 too. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01–E03]

## When checking

- **Confirmed:** Manual results after checking presentation type, exceptions, equivalence, and usability. Tags or alternative links alone cannot settle conformance. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01–E03]
- **Remaining:** Unavailable media/alternatives, missing speakers or important sounds/actions, differences from the final recording.
- **Do not overclaim:** Do not confuse an adequate alternative to audio with the exception for media that is an alternative to text. Record media/alternative references and timestamps for omissions. [Evidence: 1.2.1-E01–E03. Evidence format is our policy]
