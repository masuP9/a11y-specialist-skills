# 1.4.5 Images of Text

- Components in scope: image
- Inputs needed: image content (screenshot), name, caption, surrounding text
- Automated checks: none

## Rationale

- [Understanding 1.4.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html) ([Japanese translation](https://waic.jp/translations/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html))
  - The note on the definition of "image of text": an image in which the non-text part is significant visual content is not an image of text. The Understanding document gives charts, screenshots, and diagrams as examples.
  - The exceptions are "customizable" (the user can change the text's appearance) and "essential" (a particular presentation is essential to the information, such as logotypes).
  - The intent is that users can change the appearance of text (font, size, color, line spacing) to suit themselves.
- [C22: applying presentation with CSS](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C22), [C30: switching images of text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C30)

## Judgment lines

- Non-conformant when there is text inside the image and that text could be expressed as HTML text but is made an image instead. [Understanding]
- Images with no text are out of scope. [Understanding]
- Images that are not images of text, and images that fall under the exceptions below, are also out of scope rather than conformant. [Our interpretation] (cover images of documents in the same layout were split between conformant and non-conformant, so the handling of exceptions was unified)
- The axis of judgment is "is what the image wants to convey the content of the text (the writing), or the non-text appearance". [Understanding] (the note on the definition)
  - An image whose non-text part is significant visual content (a chart, a diagram, an image showing an app or site screen) is not an image of text even with a lot of text, and is out of scope. [Understanding]
  - A mock-up of a screen placed beside a heading to introduce a product or service (one drawing a window frame, buttons, input fields, or menus) is treated the same as an image showing a screen. [Our interpretation]
  - Even if only text appears, an image whose intent is to show the shape or arrangement of the text (a font sample, a typesetting example, handwriting, a visual comparison) is out of scope because being an image is essential. [Understanding] (essential)
  - An image that only conveys the content of the text is non-conformant: a screen capture posted to quote writing (one meant to be read, such as an article, a social media post, or the body of an email), or writing or an explanation simply turned into an image (text on a plain background). Even for a screen capture, if what is meant to be shown is only the content of the text, do not apply the exception. [Our interpretation] (how to read the note on the definition)
- Text in logos and trademarks is an exception and out of scope. [Understanding] (essential)
  - An image showing only the name of a company, organization, brand, product, or service is treated as a logo even if the typeface looks ordinary (such as company names in a list of customers or partners). Whether it is only a name is decided from the text that appears in the image, not from the name (alt). If a tagline or description accompanies the name, it is not treated as only a name. [Understanding] (the glossary defines a logotype as "text that is part of a logo or brand name". A text-only logo cannot be told apart from an image of a company name by appearance)
- Covers of documents, books, and white papers, and thumbnails (featured images) of seminars, events, and articles are exceptions and out of scope. They show the appearance of the document or event itself, so they qualify even when the only text is the title or the text is the main content. [Our interpretation] (how to read "essential")
- Expressions where a photo or illustration and text are integrated (a main image or banner with a tagline over a photo; one whose appearance could not be reproduced by making only the text HTML), signboard text that happens to appear in a photo, and small text that is part of a figure (chart tick labels) are exceptions and out of scope. The exception applies when the text is inseparable from the visual expression; writing or an explanation simply turned into an image does not qualify. [Our interpretation] (operational judgment line. Before this was written, all photo-and-tagline images were marked non-conformant)
- Which case applies is judged from the surrounding context (figure caption, surrounding text, name) and the image content (screen or window frame, UI components, chart axes and lines, how the text is arranged). Even if not indicated by the caption or name, you may judge from the image content. [Our interpretation]
- The content of the name (alt) is not a clue to whether the image contains text or is writing turned into an image. Even if the alt is a long sentence, the same as the adjacent heading or body text, or contains HTML tags, that is how the alternative text was written and does not tell what the image shows. [Our interpretation] (from an error where a product screen mock-up whose alt matched the adjacent heading was marked non-conformant as "a heading turned into an image")
- When the image cannot be viewed, do not mark non-conformant. If the name or surrounding text ("the ... screen", "screenshot", "chart of ...", "diagram of ...", "font sample", "document cover") shows it is one of the out-of-scope images above, mark out of scope; otherwise, needs review. However, if it is identified as a screen capture for quoting writing, do not mark out of scope. [Our interpretation]

## When building

- Do not make headings, buttons, or notice text into images. Express them with web fonts and CSS.
- A logo may be an image, but put the organization name in alt.
- Charts and screen captures may be images. Write the key points of the text inside in alt or the body text (1.1.1).
- For a main image that layers a tagline over a photo, make the text HTML and overlay it with CSS if possible. When not possible, put the tagline text in alt.

## When checking

- **Settled**: none.
- **Remains**: decide each image containing text, one at a time, along the axis above (content of the text, or appearance).
- **Do not assert**: do not decide it is an image of text without viewing the image. Do not mark charts, screen captures, logos (including images of only a company name), or document covers non-conformant. Do not decide it is an image of text from the wording of the alt.
