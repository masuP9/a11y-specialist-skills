# 1.4.5 Images of Text

- Components: image
- Materials: Image content (screenshot), name/caption/surrounding text
- Automated check: None
- Sources checked: 2026-11-02. WCAG: Recommendation of 2024-12-12; Understanding / Techniques: living published pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 1.4.5-E01 — Check feasibility and the two exceptions

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Images of Text](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#images-of-text), [Understanding — Intent / Examples](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Check whether the technologies in use can achieve the presentation, whether users can customize it, or whether the specific presentation is essential. Logotypes are considered essential.
- **Conditions / limits:** Includes type and typography samples where presentation itself conveys information. Production convenience alone is not essentiality. The broader treatment of name-only images, covers, and thumbnails below is author policy, not an official blanket exemption.

### 1.4.5-E02 — Separate significant visual information from text-only communication

- **Type / source:** [Normative — images of text](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-images-of-text).
- **Summary:** Pictures containing significant visual information beyond text are excluded from the definition of images of text.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not decide from file format or filename. Treatment of screenshots used solely to communicate prose is explicitly identified below as author policy.

### 1.4.5-E03 — Check the route of also providing the information as visible text

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — note in Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/images-of-text.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Providing the same information as text whose presentation users can adjust, alongside the image, meets this criterion.
- **Conditions / limits:** Alt or hidden screen-reader-only text alone does not establish this visible-text route. Do not extend this treatment to AAA criterion [1.4.9](./1.4.9.md).

### 1.4.5-E04 — Check that presentation switching works

- **Type / source:** [Technique — C22 “Description”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C22#description), [C30 “Description”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C30#description) (both Sufficient).
- **Summary:** Options include styling text with CSS or providing controls to switch from images to text.
- **Conditions / limits:** Check the alternate content and accessibility of the control. Enlarging an image alone does not necessarily allow customization to users' requirements. These are not the only permitted implementations.

## Judgment lines

- Fail when text could achieve the presentation using the technologies in use, information is conveyed only in the image, and none of the exclusions or exceptions below apply. Pass when the same information is also presented as customizable visible text. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01, E03]
- Images without text are out of scope. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E02]
- Report both pictures outside the definition and images covered by exceptions as out of scope rather than Pass. [Our interpretation: Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02. Standardize exception reporting after similarly styled covers received inconsistent verdicts]
- Judge whether the image conveys textual content or a visual appearance beyond text. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E02]
  - Graphs, diagrams, and app/site screenshots with significant non-text visual content are out of scope even when text-heavy. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E02]
  - Treat product/service UI illustrations beside headings (window frames, buttons, fields, menus) like screenshots. [Our interpretation: existing policy based on image purpose and appearance. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
  - Even text-only pictures are out of scope when showing letterforms or arrangement is essential: font samples, typography, handwriting, or visual comparisons. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01]
  - Fail images conveying only prose when equivalent visible text or another conformance route is absent: screenshots quoting articles, social posts, or email, and explanations rendered on plain backgrounds. A screenshot is not exempt when only its wording matters. [Our interpretation: reading of the definition's note. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- Logo or trademark text is excepted and reported out of scope. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01]
  - Treat images showing only a company, organization, brand, product, or service name as logos, even in ordinary-looking type, such as customer/partner lists. Decide from the visible image, not alt. Names accompanied by taglines or explanations are not name-only. [Our interpretation: default policy because text-only logos cannot reliably be distinguished by appearance. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01. The norm does not classify every company-name image as a logo]
- Covers of documents, books, and white papers, and seminar/event/article thumbnails are excepted and out of scope. They show the appearance of the item/event itself, including title-only or text-dominant images. [Our interpretation: reading of essentiality. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- Integrated photo/illustration-and-text compositions (hero images/banners whose appearance cannot be recreated by making only the text HTML), incidental sign text in photos, and small text within diagrams such as graph ticks are excepted and out of scope. This applies when text is inseparable from visual expression, not when prose is simply rasterized. [Our interpretation: operational boundary adopted after all photo-and-copy compositions were being failed. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- Decide using context (figure caption, surrounding prose, name) and image content (windows, UI, graph axes/lines, text layout). The image itself may establish the purpose even without a caption or name explaining it. [Our interpretation: existing policy based on image purpose and appearance. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- Do not use alt wording to infer that an image contains text or rasterized prose. Long alt, duplicated adjacent headings/prose, or HTML tags describe alternative-text authoring, not pixels. [Our interpretation: after a product UI illustration was wrongly failed because its alt duplicated a heading. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- If the image cannot be viewed, do not fail it. If its name/context establishes an excluded purpose such as a screen, graph, font sample, or cover, report out of scope; otherwise review. A screenshot known to quote prose is not excluded. [Our interpretation: existing policy based on image purpose and appearance. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- Do not flag user-customizable images or an accessible switch to equivalent text. Report the customization exception as out of scope under existing policy, and the text-view switching route as Pass. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01, E04. Reporting categories are ours]

## Judgment examples

- **Pass:** An announcement image also appears as adjacent visible text with adjustable presentation. Distinguish this from alt alone. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E03]
- **Pass:** An accessible presentation switch replaces a heading image with equivalent HTML text. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E04]
- **Out of scope:** A specimen image comparing typefaces; letterforms themselves convey information. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01. Reporting category is ours]
- **Out of scope (author policy):** A cover of the document being introduced or customer-name-only images. Apply the defaults above; abstract uncertainty alone does not return these to review. [Our interpretation: examples of existing policy. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E02]
- **Fail:** Ordinary explanatory prose is provided only as an image on a plain background. Alt exists, but visible text, switching, and exceptions are absent. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01, E03–E04]
- **Needs review:** An unavailable image has long prose alt, but its purpose is unknown. Obtain the image, surrounding presentation, and any equivalent visible text. [Our interpretation: inspection example. Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E03]

## When building

- Use web fonts and CSS for headings, buttons, and notices instead of images.
- Logos may be images; put the organization's name in alt.
- Graphs and screenshots may be images; put their key information in alt or body text (1.1.1).
- Prefer HTML text overlaid with CSS for hero images with copy. If that cannot be done, include the copy in alt.

## When checking

- **What automation settles:** Nothing. Manual inspection of the image, purpose, and conformance routes can settle the verdict. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E04]
- **Remaining:** Assess each image containing text using the distinction above (wording versus appearance), equivalent visible text, and presentation switching. Adequate alt does not substitute for meeting 1.4.5. [Evidence: 1.4.5-E01–E04]
- **Do not overclaim:** Do not classify an image as an image of text without viewing it. Do not fail graphs, screenshots, logos (including name-only images), or document covers under the boundaries above. Do not infer images of text from alt wording.
