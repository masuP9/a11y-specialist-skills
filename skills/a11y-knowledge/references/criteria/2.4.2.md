# 2.4.2 Page Titled

- Scope: whole page (implementation and tool coverage here focuses on HTML)
- Inputs needed: `document.title`, current content, headings, application purpose, and post-navigation states where needed
- Automated checks: axe `document-title` checks that the title is non-empty
- Sources checked: 2026-11-02. WCAG is the 2024-12-12 Recommendation; Understanding / Techniques are living WCAG 2.2 public pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Rationale

### 2.4.2-E01 — Judge whether the title describes topic or purpose

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Page Titled](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#page-titled), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/page-titled.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Compare the title with current content and purpose. A document or web application name can sufficiently describe purpose. When an SPA changes to a distinct page/view, check that the title reflects the current content.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not require string equality with h1. Do not require title updates for every expansion or selection; assess whether the current topic or purpose is described.

### 2.4.2-E02 — Separate non-empty titles from descriptive titles

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G88, Description and Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G88.html#tests), [H25, Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H25#tests) (Sufficient together for HTML).
- **Summary:** Check both the HTML title element and whether its content identifies the page. G88 treats site names and uniqueness as helpful additions.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not make site-name presence, separators, order, or character counts independent failure conditions. For duplicate titles, inspect whether each page is described rather than failing duplication alone.

### 2.4.2-E03 — Compare defaults, filenames, and duplicates with content

- **Type / source:** [Failure — F25, Description, Examples, and Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F25#tests).
- **Summary:** Defaults such as untitled, non-descriptive filenames, empty text, and filler fail when they do not identify content or purpose. Also check identification in the example of identical template-generated titles.
- **Conditions / limits:** Filename format alone does not fail. Distinguish allowing identical titles for paginated search results or identical content at different URLs from masking unrelated content with one site name.

## Judgment lines

- A site name alone conforms when the homepage's topic is the site itself. Allow document/application names that describe purpose, without exempting all views merely because the application is named. [Evidence: 2.4.2-E01–E03]
- Fail clear topic mismatches; do not return to needs review through unsupported speculation that the title might describe another purpose. Compare body content and application purpose too, rather than deciding from h1 alone. [Our interpretation: author policy avoiding misjudgments. Evidence: 2.4.2-E01, E03]
- Duplicate titles are investigation clues. They do not fail solely for duplication when topic or purpose is described. [Evidence: 2.4.2-E02–E03]
- axe-core 4.14.0 `document-title` checks `document.title` after normalizing whitespace and similar content. Treat violations establishing missing/empty HTML page titles as failures, but do not treat passes as descriptive-title judgments. [Our interpretation: tool mapping. Implementation checked: `docHasTitleEvaluate` in `node_modules/axe-core/axe.js`. Evidence: 2.4.2-E02]

## Judgment examples

- **Conformant:** A homepage carries the site name, or a document carries its document name. Do not flag solely for non-identical title and h1 wording. [Evidence: 2.4.2-E01–E02]
- **Non-conformant:** Content and headings explain waste sorting while the title is an unrelated “Contact us.” Even if many pages share the title, judge each against its content. [Our interpretation: author application example. Evidence: 2.4.2-E01, E03]
- **Do not flag:** Paginated results of the same search share a title describing the search purpose. Adding page numbers remains a recommendation. [Our interpretation: duplicate-title example. Evidence: 2.4.2-E01–E02]
- **Needs review:** The title is available, but authenticated content or the changed SPA view is not. Obtain content and title for the same target state. [Our interpretation: inspection policy. Evidence: 2.4.2-E01]

## When building

- Prefer topic first, followed by the site name as needed. Pagination numbers improve identification but are not universally required. [Our interpretation: author formatting recommendation. Supporting evidence: 2.4.2-E02]
- When navigating to a distinct SPA page/view, provide a title matching the current topic or purpose. [Evidence: 2.4.2-E01]

## When checking

- **Settled:** Missing/empty HTML page titles or clear mismatches with current topic/purpose. [2.4.2-E01–E03]
- **Remains:** Content comparisons, each page with a duplicate title, and SPA states. Do not decide title conformance by majority.
- **Do not assert:** Failure solely from duplication, missing site names, length, or differences from h1. [Evidence: 2.4.2-E01–E03]
