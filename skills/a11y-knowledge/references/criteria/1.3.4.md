# 1.3.4 Orientation

- Scope: restrictions on page display and operation orientation
- Inputs needed: portrait/landscape display and operation, orientation controls, and essentiality rationale
- Automated checks: orientation-check (display differences and rotate-message candidates)

- Sources checked: 2026-11-01. WCAG refers to the 2024-12-12 Recommendation; Understanding / Techniques are the continuously updated WCAG 2.2 published pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 1.3.4-E01 — Separate author restrictions from display size and user settings

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — “Intent” / “Locking a device to an orientation”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/orientation.html#intent), [Failure — F100 “Description”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F100#description).
- **Summary:** Check whether the author restricts display/operation to one orientation, not merely whether orientation is detected. Distinguish user-selected OS locks.
- **Conditions / limits:** Different layouts alone do not fail. Evaluate content/function changes due to display size separately. A detected rotate message requires confirmation that it actually prevents using the content.

### 1.3.4-E02 — Check controls for changing content orientation

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G214 “Description” / “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G214.html#tests) (Sufficient), [Failure — F97 “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F97#tests).
- **Summary:** Even without automatic rotation, a control can let users change content orientation and use their preferred orientation.
- **Conditions / limits:** Merely asking users to physically rotate the device is not an alternative. Verify display and use after operating the control, not just its presence.

### 1.3.4-E03 — Check examples and reasons for an essential orientation

- **Type / source:** [Normative — WCAG 2.2 “Orientation”](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#orientation), [Understanding — “Intent” / “Examples”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/orientation.html#examples).
- **Summary:** Checks and piano apps are examples where orientation can be essential; verify whether the actual information, function, or environment requires that orientation.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not exempt something merely because it is an instrument or game. Match technical orientation constraints such as TV or VR to the actual environment.

## Judgment boundaries

- Fail when the author restricts display/operation in one orientation, switching mechanisms do not make it usable, and the restriction is not essential. [Evidence: 1.3.4-E01–E03]
- orientation-check opens the page separately at 375×667 and 667×375 viewports, inferring restrictions from wording and main-region dimensions/visibility. It does not test physical rotation, OS locks, or switching controls. Candidates require review; no-finding passes remain limited to the inspected scope. [Our interpretation: tool mapping. Implementation: [runOrientationCheck.ts](../../../../packages/a11y-audit/src/playwright/runOrientationCheck.ts), [normalizeOrientationCheck](../../../../packages/a11y-audit/src/utils/axe-format.ts)]

## Judgment examples

- Landscape locking to capture a wide paper check accurately, or to preserve a piano keyboard’s relative physical layout, usable key widths, and number of keys, are essential examples in Understanding. When the function and constraints match, do not flag the orientation lock alone. [Evidence: 1.3.4-E03]
- Content for a television available in only one orientation, or VR goggles with screens fixed relative to the eyes, can qualify through those technical constraints. Distinguish these from locking merely because something is a game or has a landscape design. [Evidence: 1.3.4-E03]

## When building

- Prefer adapting to the user's orientation and respecting OS lock settings. Do not prohibit orientation media queries themselves; avoid making content unusable in one orientation. [Evidence: 1.3.4-E01, 1.3.4-E02]

## When checking

- **Established:** Actual display/operation restrictions, switching mechanisms, and essentiality. [1.3.4-E01–E03]
- **Remaining:** Physical orientation changes, content after switching, app-specific states, and distinctions from OS settings.
- **Do not conclude:** Wording or viewport differences alone do not establish a lock. Do not declare conformance without measurement. [Our interpretation: inspection policy]
