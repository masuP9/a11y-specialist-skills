# 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold

- Scope: All content on the page, including content not relied upon for conformance (ads, embeds, parts excluded from conformance)
- Materials: What flashes, the number of light/dark transitions per second, the area flashing at the same time, the colors of the light and dark states (relative luminance, degree of red), the size at the largest scale it is viewed, and whether it loops
- Automated check: None. axe-core 4.14.0 has no rule that measures flashing (`blink` / `marquee` are tagged `wcag222` and are for 2.2.2). `@a11y-skills/audit` has none either. `auto-play-detection` reports moving content as candidates but does not measure the number, area, or luminance of flashes
- Sources checked: 2026-11-10. WCAG: Recommendation of 2024-12-12. Understanding updated 2025-09-17; G19, G176, and G15 updated 2025-07-15 (living published pages). The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 2.3.1-E01 — Applies to all content

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Three Flashes or Below Threshold](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#three-flashes-or-below-threshold), [Conformance requirement 5 — Non-Interference](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#cc5), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Nothing flashes more than three times in any one-second period, or the flash is below the general flash and red flash thresholds. Content that fails this can interfere with use of the whole page, so it applies to all content on the page, including content not relied upon for conformance. Flashing cannot be allowed even for a second, and a way to turn it off is not a solution (a seizure can occur before the user turns it off).
- **Conditions / limits:** Flicker caused by the display or the computer is out of scope; the criterion covers flashing caused by the content itself. Blinking, which is a distraction problem, is assessed under [2.2.2](./2.2.2.md); at three or more per second it is also a flash.

### 2.3.1-E02 — Decide by count

- **Type / source:** [Term — general flash and red flash thresholds](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-general-flash-and-red-flash-thresholds), [Term — flash](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-flashes), [Technique — G19 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G19#description) (Sufficient).
- **Summary:** If there are no more than three general flashes and no more than three red flashes in any one-second period, it is below the threshold. A flash is a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance (two transitions, light to dark or dark to light). Three flashes are six transitions; seven transitions (3.5 flashes) exceed it. Definition note 4 says content flashing three times or fewer per second passes without an analysis tool.
- **Conditions / limits:** G19 keeps flashing at three or fewer regardless of area; it is stricter than the criterion but easier to test. The note is informative under section 5.1, but the count condition itself is in the body of the definition.

### 2.3.1-E03 — Decide by area

- **Type / source:** [Term — general flash and red flash thresholds](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-general-flash-and-red-flash-thresholds), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html#intent), [Technique — G176 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G176#description) (Sufficient).
- **Summary:** If the combined area of concurrent flashes is no more than 25% of any 10 degree visual field, it is below the threshold. Understanding expresses the 10 degree field as 341 × 256 CSS px (87,296 CSS px), and 25% of that is 21,824 CSS px. G176 passes a single flashing event (with no other flashing on screen) of any shape whose contiguous area is smaller than 21,824 pixels. Analyze content at the largest scale a user may view it, at the user agent's standard zoom level. Analyze video that can also play full screen at full screen. Analyze short clips that loop (such as GIFs) while looping.
- **Conditions / limits:** The area is the total of what flashes concurrently and contiguously. A note acknowledges that users closer to the screen, or using zoom, are affected by flashing areas that normatively pass.

### 2.3.1-E04 — Luminance and red conditions

- **Type / source:** [Term — general flash and red flash thresholds](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-general-flash-and-red-flash-thresholds), [Term — relative luminance](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-relative-luminance), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html#intent).
- **Summary:** A general flash is a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum (1.0), where the darker state is below 0.80. A red flash is any pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated red. A WCAG 2.2 note gives the new working definition in the field: one state has R/(R+G+B) of 0.8 or more and the difference between states exceeds 0.2 in the CIE 1976 UCS chromaticity diagram. Fine, balanced patterns (a checkerboard with squares smaller than 0.1 degree of visual field on a side, white noise) do not violate the thresholds. Video in color spaces other than sRGB is tested in the version with the highest dynamic range.
- **Conditions / limits:** The working definition of a red flash is a note and is informative.

### 2.3.1-E05 — Tool checks and warnings

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G15 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G15#description) (Sufficient), [Understanding — Examples](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html#examples).
- **Summary:** The simple test in G19 applies to three or fewer flashes per second, and the one in G176 to a single small flashing area. Everything else needs an analysis tool that tracks all the factors over time. Warnings can be provided, but people may miss them and children may not be able to read them. Examples are video of muzzle flashes limited to an area below the threshold, and a movie whose lightning is edited to flash only three times in any one second.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not treat a particular tool's result as the only way to judge.

## Judgment lines

- Pass if there are three or fewer flashes (six or fewer light/dark transitions) in any one-second period. Do not measure area or luminance. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E02]
- Even above three, pass if the combined area flashing at the same time is 21,824 CSS px (25%) or less within any 341 × 256 CSS px region. Check video that can play full screen at full-screen size, and looping video while looping. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E03]
- If it is above three and the area exceeds the threshold, check the general flash and red flash conditions with the colors of the light and dark states. Calculate CSS or JS animations and GIFs from the specified colors or each frame's colors. For video, measure two frames: the brightest and the darkest. Fail if either condition is met; pass if neither is. If it cannot be measured, mark it as needing review and check it with an analysis tool (PEAT or similar). [Author policy: the two-frame procedure when no tool is available. Evidence: 2.3.1-E04, E05]
- Treat flashing of fine, balanced patterns (less than 0.1 degree of visual field on a side) as not exceeding the thresholds. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E04]
- Do not exempt video that plays only after pressing play, warnings before playback, or content that stops only when `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled. Fail if the default presentation flashes above the thresholds. [Author policy: treatment of reduced-motion settings. The criterion has no exception, conformance requirement 5 applies it to all content, and G15 says warnings can be missed. Evidence: 2.3.1-E01, E05]
- Also fail flashing in ads, embedded video, and parts excluded from conformance. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E01]

## Judgment examples

- **Pass:** A notification badge blinks once per second. The count alone decides it (check it separately under 2.2.2 as blinking). [Evidence: 2.3.1-E02]
- **Pass:** A 100 × 100 CSS px (10,000 CSS px) icon flashes five times per second, with nothing else flashing at the same time. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E03]
- **Fail:** A CSS animation switches the whole page background between white and black five times per second. The difference between white (relative luminance 1.0) and black (0.0) far exceeds 10%, and the darker state is below 0.80. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E02–E04]
- **Fail:** In an explosion scene of a video that can play full screen, most of the screen switches between white and dark red four times per second. Pressing play first does not exempt it. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E01, E04]
- **Needs review:** In a night-scene video, most of the screen brightens slightly four times per second. The relative luminance difference between the two frames is around 10%, and compression makes the values unstable. Check with an analysis tool. [Author policy: inspection example. Evidence: 2.3.1-E04, E05]

## When building

- If something flashes, keep it to three or fewer times in any one-second period. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E02]
- If more than three flashes are needed, keep the flashing area small and the luminance difference small. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E03, E04]
- Check video with an analysis tool before publishing and edit scenes that exceed the thresholds. Do not rely on a warning alone. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E05]

## When checking

- **What automation settles:** Nothing. Differences from `auto-play-detection` are candidates for moving content and do not decide the number, area, or luminance of flashes. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E02–E04]
- **Settled:** Results of checking the number of transitions per second, the area flashing at the same time, and the light and dark colors. For video, keep the playback time and scene as evidence. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E02–E04]
- **Remaining:** Video scenes not played during the audit, and content that appears only after login or interaction. State the range played in the report. [Author policy: inspection scope]
- **Do not assert:** Do not judge video from a single still image. Do not use a way to stop it, or a warning, as grounds for a pass. [Evidence: 2.3.1-E01, E05]
