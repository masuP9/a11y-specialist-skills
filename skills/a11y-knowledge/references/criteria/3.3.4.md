# 3.3.4 Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data)

- Scope: functions causing legal/financial consequences, changing/deleting stored user-controllable data, or submitting test responses
- Inputs needed: consequences, steps through commitment, reversal, validation/correction, and confirmation mechanisms
- Automated checks: none; form elements and confirmation-button names alone do not settle this
- Sources checked: 2026-11-02. WCAG is the 2024-12-12 Recommendation; Understanding / Techniques are living public pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Rationale

### 3.3.4-E01 — Check consequences and the three conformance paths

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data)](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#error-prevention-legal-financial-data), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html#intent).
- **Summary:** For in-scope actions, verify at least one of reversal, input-error checking with correction, or review/confirmation/correction before commitment.
- **Conditions / limits:** The intent is not confirmation dialogs for every save or simple document creation/edit. Distinguish internal logs users cannot view/change from user-controllable data. Confirmation screens are not the only method.

### 3.3.4-E02 — Verify review and correction of the whole submission before commitment

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G98, Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G98#tests), [G168, Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G168#tests) (Sufficient in their applicable situations).
- **Summary:** Multi-step processes need access to review/correct earlier input. Confirmations for deletion and similar actions should explain the action/consequences and allow cancellation.
- **Conditions / limits:** A separate confirmation page is not required; editable information and confirmation on one screen can suffice. “Go to confirmation” alone does not prove review/correction is available.

### 3.3.4-E03 — Check confirmation-checkbox behavior

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G155, Description / Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G155#tests) (Sufficient).
- **Summary:** Use an initially unchecked confirmation checkbox separate from submission. Without selection, prevent commitment and prompt review/selection without losing input.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not call G168 the checkbox technique. Mere presence, preselection, or proceeding unchecked does not establish G155. Still check other conformance paths.

### 3.3.4-E04 — Verify usable reversal/recovery methods and conditions

- **Type / source:** [Technique — G99, Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G99#tests), [G164, Description / Tests](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G164#tests) (Sufficient in their applicable situations).
- **Summary:** Check recovery of deleted information and stated periods/procedures for amending or canceling transactions.
- **Conditions / limits:** Cancellation need not happen on the same site. For another mechanism, check advance notice that online cancellation is unavailable and equivalent accessibility across disabilities. Do not impose example periods such as 24 hours universally.

## Judgment lines

- Determine scope from consequences, not form names. Ordinary search/contact/login without in-scope consequences is not applicable. A “survey” submitting test answers is in scope; assess stored-comment deletion as its own action. Do not miss deletion buttons outside forms. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01; prioritizing consequences over names is an author inspection example]
- Any verified conformance path suffices. Without a confirmation screen, inspect validation/correction or reversal/recovery before failing. Retain needs review when the initial screen does not reveal the mechanism. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E04; initial-screen treatment is author inspection policy]
- Do not pass from button/checkbox names alone; verify the information, correction method, and behavior preventing commitment. Fail an in-scope action when none of the paths is available. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E04]
- The checked path requires an opportunity to correct, not only error detection. It does not require inferring every user intention. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01]

## Judgment examples

- **Conformant:** Users can review and correct all purchase information before committing the order, without a separate confirmation page. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E02]
- **Conformant through another path:** No deletion confirmation dialog appears, but deleted records can be recovered. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E04]
- **Non-conformant:** No reversal/recovery or validation/correction exists, and a preselected “Confirmed” checkbox allows an in-scope submission to commit without review/correction. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E03]
- **Not applicable:** Submitting a search query without an in-scope transaction, data change, or test response. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01]
- **Needs review:** “Go to confirmation” exists, but its destination and cancellation conditions were not obtained. Inspect steps before commitment and correction/reversal methods. [Our interpretation: inspection example. Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E04]

## When building

- Select a conformance path appropriate to the consequences, making review/correction or reversal/recovery usable. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E04]
- For checkbox confirmation, prevent unconfirmed submissions; for deletion confirmation, explain the target and consequences. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E02–E03]

## When checking

- **Settled:** In-scope consequences and availability of at least one conformance path, or confirmed absence of all paths. [3.3.4-E01–E04]
- **Remains:** Precommitment review/correction, unchecked behavior, cancellation periods, and recoverability.
- **Do not assert:** Fail from missing confirmation screens or Pass from button names/checkbox presence alone. [Evidence: 3.3.4-E01–E04]
