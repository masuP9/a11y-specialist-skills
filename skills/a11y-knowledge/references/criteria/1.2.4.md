# 1.2.4 Captions (Live)

- Scope: Live audio in synchronized media. Broadcasts that a presenter streams to many people (live streams, webinars)
- Materials: Whether the stream is live, whether it has both audio and video, the form of the stream (one-way or two-way), the screen during the stream or a recording that keeps the captions shown at broadcast time, and the caption content (dialogue, speakers, important sounds) and presentation
- Automated check: None. axe-core 4.14.0 has no rule for live captions (`video-caption` is tagged `wcag122` and is for 1.2.2). `@a11y-skills/audit` has none either
- Sources checked: 2026-11-10. WCAG: Recommendation of 2024-12-12. Understanding updated 2026-03-09; G9 updated 2025-09-17; G93 updated 2025-07-15; G87 updated 2026-02-23 (living published pages). The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 1.2.4-E01 — Apply to live synchronized media

- **Type / source:** [Normative — Captions (Live)](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#captions-live), [Term — live](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-live), [Term — synchronized media](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-synchronized-media).
- **Summary:** Captions are provided for all live audio content in synchronized media. Live means information captured from a real-world event and transmitted with no more than a broadcast delay (a short delay to cue or censor, not enough for significant editing). Information that is completely computer generated is not live.
- **Conditions / limits:** A live audio-only stream with no video is outside this criterion (AAA 1.2.9). A prerecorded video streamed at a scheduled time (a premiere, for example) does not deliver a real-world event as it happens, so it is not live; assess it under [1.2.2](./1.2.2.md). A recording of a stream published afterwards is also assessed under 1.2.2.

### 1.2.4-E02 — Separate broadcasts from two-way calls

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-live.html#intent).
- **Summary:** The criterion applies to broadcasts of synchronized media. It does not require that two-way multimedia calls between two or more people through web apps be captioned regardless of the users' needs. Responsibility for captions falls to the content providers (the callers) or the host, not the application.
- **Conditions / limits:** It does not address settings that mix a one-way broadcast with participant speech, such as a webinar with Q&A or chat.

### 1.2.4-E03 — What captions need to convey

- **Type / source:** [Term — captions](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-captions), [Understanding — Intent](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-live.html#intent), [Understanding — Examples](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-live.html#examples).
- **Summary:** Captions convey not only dialogue but also who is speaking and important sounds such as sound effects. Examples are a captioned news web cast, and an orchestra web performance with CART captioning that conveys lyrics and dialogue and identifies non-vocal music by title, movement, and composer.
- **Conditions / limits:** No number is given for caption accuracy or acceptable delay.

### 1.2.4-E04 — How to provide them and how they are shown

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — Sufficient Techniques](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-live.html#sufficient), [Technique — G9 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G9#description) (Sufficient together with G93 or G87), [G93 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G93#description), [G87 "Description"](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G87#description).
- **Summary:** G9 (creating live captions) combined with G93 (open, always visible captions) or G87 (closed captions) is sufficient. Captions may be generated with a real-time text translation service. G9 lists stenographic, rapid typing, and re-voicing techniques and notes that real-time captions are hard to make accurate because there is little time to correct mistakes.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not fail for not using a Technique alone. The sources say neither that automatic speech recognition is sufficient nor that it is insufficient.

## Judgment lines

- A live stream with video and audio fails if it has no captions during the stream. Streams without audio, audio-only streams, and playback of prerecorded media are out of scope; the latter two are assessed under 1.2.9 (AAA) and 1.2.2 respectively. [Evidence: 1.2.4-E01]
- The part a presenter streams to many people is in scope, including webinars with Q&A or chat. Meeting and call features among participants (the web conferencing app itself) are out of scope. [Author policy: the line for settings that mix a one-way broadcast with participant speech. Their main content is close to the web cast in the Understanding example. Evidence: 1.2.4-E02]
- Judge caption content by whether dialogue, speakers, and important sounds come through. Do not decide by whether it is automatic speech recognition or human transcription. Set no line in seconds for delay, and do not fail for delay alone. Fail when you confirm gaps such as continued omissions or errors that change the meaning, or speakers that cannot be identified. [Author policy: not deciding by the method of generation or by delay, the same as the existing 1.2.2 policy. Evidence: 1.2.4-E03, E04]
- If there is no stream at audit time, judge from recordings of past streams. If a recording keeps the captions shown at broadcast time, also treat future streams on the same streaming setup as passing. [Author policy: the W3C requirement is that each stream itself has captions, and captions on past streams do not guarantee captions on future ones. Because audits rarely get to observe a stream, we substitute the past record of the same setup. State this assumption and the recordings checked in the report. Evidence: 1.2.4-E01]
- The assumption above applies only when the recording's captions can be confirmed as those shown at broadcast time. Material that confirms this: open captions kept in the recording, a broadcast-time caption track kept on the streaming platform, or records of the stream settings or captioning service. If they cannot be distinguished from captions added afterwards, do not use them as grounds for a pass; keep it as needing review. [Author policy: the line for material supporting the assumption]
- If you confirm that a recording of a past stream has no broadcast-time captions (the broadcast-time screen is kept and shows no captions), that stream fails. Also report it as the expectation for future streams. [Author policy: using the assumption on the failing side too]
- Do not use the assumption from past recordings for a stream whose setup (platform, captioning method, host) differs from the past streams. [Author policy: the extent of the assumption]
- If there is neither a screen during the stream nor a recording usable for the assumption, it needs review. Record the date and time of the next stream and what to check (presence of captions, content, presentation). Do not pass on a "captions provided" notice alone. [Author policy: inspection policy. Evidence: 1.2.4-E01, E03]

## Judgment examples

- **Pass:** A live product announcement shows CART captions at all times, with speaker names and "(applause)". [Evidence: 1.2.4-E03, E04]
- **Pass:** Automatic speech recognition captions lag by a few seconds, but the meaning of the dialogue is kept, and speakers can be identified from on-screen names and the caption notation. [Author policy: treatment of method of generation and delay. Evidence: 1.2.4-E03]
- **Fail:** A webinar with Q&A has no captions for either the talk or the Q&A. [Author policy: the line for mixed settings. Evidence: 1.2.4-E02]
- **Fail:** Automatic captions keep misrecognizing technical terms, so the explanation of the procedure does not come through. [Author policy. Evidence: 1.2.4-E03]
- **Out of scope:** A call in a web conferencing app used for internal meetings. A live radio stream (audio only). A premiere of a prerecorded video (assess under 1.2.2). [Evidence: 1.2.4-E01, E02]
- **Pass (assumed):** There is no stream at audit time, but the archive of the previous stream, run on the same platform with the same captioning service, keeps the broadcast-time caption track. [Author policy: the assumption. Evidence: 1.2.4-E01]
- **Needs review:** The archive of the previous stream has captions, but it is unclear whether they were redone after the stream. Check the stream settings or the captioning service's records. [Author policy: the line for material supporting the assumption]

## When building

- Provide live streams with human transcription such as CART, or with automatic captions backed by a setup that can also supply speakers and important sounds. [Evidence: 1.2.4-E03, E04]
- With closed captions, make sure users can find how to turn them on. Open captions are also fine. [Evidence: 1.2.4-E04]
- When publishing a recording of a stream, correct its content as captions under 1.2.2. [Evidence: 1.2.4-E01]

## When checking

- **What automation settles:** Nothing. Do not carry axe `video-caption` results over to 1.2.4. [Evidence: 1.2.4-E01]
- **Settled:** Results of checking the presence, content, and presentation of captions on the screen during the stream, or in a recording usable for the assumption in the judgment lines. [Evidence: 1.2.4-E01, E03]
- **Remaining:** Captions for each stream when there is no stream and no recording. When passed by assumption, the fact that each stream itself was not checked. [Author policy: limit of the assumption]
- **Do not assert:** Do not pass on a "captions provided" notice or terms of use alone. Do not infer broadcast-time captions from captions added to a recording afterwards. [Author policy. Evidence: 1.2.4-E01]
