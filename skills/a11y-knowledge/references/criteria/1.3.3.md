# 1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics

- Scope: text across the whole page (instructions and explanatory sentences). Not attached to components
- Inputs needed: image of the whole page (the instruction text and how the referenced component looks)
- Automated checks: none

- Sources checked: 2026-11-01. Understanding / Techniques are the continuously updated WCAG 2.2 published pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 1.3.3-E01 — Check for identification beyond sensory descriptions

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — “Intent” / “Examples”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/sensory-characteristics.html#intent), [Technique — G96 “Description” / “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G96.html#tests) (Sufficient), [Failure — F14 “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F14#tests).
- **Summary:** Shape and location can supplement instructions. Check whether function, label, heading, or other information identifies the target without requiring perception of sensory characteristics.
- **Conditions / limits:** G96 does not make verbatim repetition of a visible label the only permitted method. Distinguish that from this knowledge base's policy favoring references by name.

### 1.3.3-E02 — Check reading order and ambiguity even for “below”

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — “Intent”, above / below discussion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/sensory-characteristics.html#intent).
- **Summary:** In languages where above/below refers to document sequence, the reference conforms when the target is in the corresponding reading-order position and the reference is unambiguous.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not exclude references merely because they say “below” or “above”. Retain review when visual and reading order differ or the target is ambiguous.

### 1.3.3-E03 — Color plus shape can still depend entirely on sensory information

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — “Examples”, Example 1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/sensory-characteristics.html#examples), [Failure — F26 “Description” / “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F26#tests).
- **Summary:** Instructions identifying venues solely by color and shape are a failure example. For instructions referring to symbols, check for another means of determining their information.
- **Conditions / limits:** Adding shape to color can satisfy 1.4.1 without satisfying 1.3.3. Text alternatives for symbols can also be evidence.

## Judgment lines

- What to look for: among instruction or explanatory sentences for operating or understanding content, those that refer to a thing solely by shape, size, visual location, orientation, color, or sound ("press the button on the right", "confirm with the red button", "press the round icon", "enter in the left field", "move on when the sound plays"). [Evidence: 1.3.3-E01]
- If the referenced thing is also indicated by the name, label, or heading text visible on screen, conformant ("the 'Submit' button on the right", "the magnifying glass icon (Search)"). Prioritize naming the visible label in instructions; a name on the component alone does not compensate for inadequate instructions. [Our interpretation: author's judgment policy, not the only normative method. Supporting evidence: 1.3.3-E01]
- Do not list “the following”, “below”, or “as in the table above” when the target is in the corresponding reading-order position and unambiguous. Separately check references such as left/right or screen corners that cannot be determined from reading order. [Evidence: 1.3.3-E02]
- For paired instructions ("The upper button is for members. The lower one is for first-time visitors."), list each sentence as a separate location rather than merging them. Do not list only the first sentence and drop the second. [Our interpretation: author's inspection/reporting policy. Supporting evidence: 1.3.3-E01] (from the pattern of misses)
- Instructions that announce when to act by sound alone ("stop answering when the chime sounds") are listed even without a referenced thing. If the same cue is confirmed to appear on screen, the instruction does not depend on sound alone. [Evidence: 1.3.3-E01]
- Do not list: descriptions that are not instructions (sentences that convey the color or shape itself, like "a photo of red roses"), appearances that convey information by color alone (red labels, chart series) and notes explaining the meaning of a color (these are 1.4.1). [Our interpretation: reporting division with 1.4.1. Reporting color-meaning notes under 1.4.1 is our policy, not a general exclusion from 1.3.3. Supporting evidence: 1.3.3-E03]

## When building

- When referring to a component in an instruction, write the name visible on that component ("press the 'Submit' button"). Adding position or color is fine.
- "Left" and "right" change meaning when the layout changes with screen width. Referring by name does not break.
- If cueing by sound, also show the same cue on screen (text, icon).
- Refer to input fields by the field's name ("in the 'Postal code' field, without a hyphen").

## When checking

- **Settled**: none. Automated checks cannot read instruction sentences.
- **Remains**: read instructions and check target identification, symbol alternatives, and reading order. Color plus shape alone is not necessarily sufficient. [1.3.3-E01–E03]
- **Do not assert**: do not mark "the following" that only refers to the document's sequence non-conformant. Do not mark descriptions that are not instructions non-conformant.
