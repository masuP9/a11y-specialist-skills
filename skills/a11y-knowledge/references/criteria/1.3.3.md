# 1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics

- Scope: text across the whole page (instructions and explanatory sentences). Not attached to components
- Inputs needed: image of the whole page (the instruction text and how the referenced component looks)
- Automated checks: none

## Rationale

- [Understanding 1.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/sensory-characteristics.html) ([Japanese translation](https://waic.jp/translations/WCAG22/Understanding/sensory-characteristics.html))
  - The scope is "instructions for understanding and operating content". The instructions must not rely solely on shape, size, visual location, orientation, or sound.
  - Using sensory characteristics is not itself prohibited. It is fine if also indicated in text ("the 'Submit' button on the right").
  - Conveying information by color alone is the range of 1.4.1. However, instructions that refer to a thing to press by color, like "press the red button", are also checked here.
- [G96: provide textual identification in addition to sensory characteristics](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G96), [F14: identifying by shape or location only](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F14), [F26: identifying by the shape of a symbol only](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F26)

## Judgment lines

- What to look for: among instruction or explanatory sentences for operating or understanding content, those that refer to a thing solely by shape, size, visual location, orientation, color, or sound ("press the button on the right", "confirm with the red button", "press the round icon", "enter in the left field", "move on when the sound plays"). [Understanding]
- If the referenced thing is also indicated by the name, label, or heading text visible on screen, conformant ("the 'Submit' button on the right", "the magnifying glass icon (Search)"). Even if the referenced component has a name, if the instruction sentence does not say that name, non-conformant. [Understanding]
- Do not list expressions that only refer to the document's sequence (reading order), such as "the following", "below", "as in the table above", "in the next steps"; these still make sense in reading order. List those that refer to a position not determinable in reading order, such as left/right, a corner of the screen, or a position in a horizontal row. [Understanding] (the note on "above / below" in Understanding)
- For paired instructions ("The upper button is for members. The lower one is for first-time visitors."), list each sentence as a separate location rather than merging them. Do not list only the first sentence and drop the second. [Our interpretation] (from the pattern of misses)
- Instructions that announce when to act by sound alone ("stop answering when the chime sounds") are listed even without a referenced thing. If it says the same cue also appears on screen, conformant. [Understanding]
- Do not list: descriptions that are not instructions (sentences that convey the color or shape itself, like "a photo of red roses"), appearances that convey information by color alone (red labels, chart series) and notes explaining the meaning of a color (these are 1.4.1). [Our interpretation] (division with 1.4.1)

## When building

- When referring to a component in an instruction, write the name visible on that component ("press the 'Submit' button"). Adding position or color is fine.
- "Left" and "right" change meaning when the layout changes with screen width. Referring by name does not break.
- If cueing by sound, also show the same cue on screen (text, icon).
- Refer to input fields by the field's name ("in the 'Postal code' field, without a hyphen").

## When checking

- **Settled**: none. Automated checks cannot read instruction sentences.
- **Remains**: read all instruction sentences and confirm, one sentence at a time, whether the referenced thing is also indicated by name.
- **Do not assert**: do not mark "the following" that only refers to the document's sequence non-conformant. Do not mark descriptions that are not instructions non-conformant.
