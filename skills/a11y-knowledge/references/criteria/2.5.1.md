# 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures

- Scope: author-implemented multipoint and path-based interactions
- Inputs needed: pointer-gesture-check candidates and results of operating single-pointer alternatives
- Automated checks: pointer-gesture-check (candidate collection only)

- Sources checked: 2026-11-01. WCAG refers to the 2024-12-12 Recommendation; Understanding / Techniques are the continuously updated WCAG 2.2 published pages. The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Evidence

### 2.5.1-E01 — Distinguish dragging from path constraints

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — “Intent”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Check whether the intermediate path, direction, or similar constraints determine whether the interaction succeeds. Distinguish dragging where only the start and end points matter and the intermediate route is unrestricted.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not decide from a component name such as slider or carousel. Even without a path constraint, check dragging under [2.5.7](./2.5.7.md).

### 2.5.1-E02 — Keyboard support alone is not an alternative

- **Type / source:** [Failure — F105 “Description” / “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F105#tests), [Technique — G215 “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G215.html#tests), [G216 “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G216.html#tests) (both techniques are Sufficient).
- **Summary:** Check that a single pointer can perform the same function without a path-based gesture. Previous/next buttons or clicking a slider track can provide alternatives. Keyboard-only alternatives are insufficient.
- **Conditions / limits:** Do not require exactly one click. Double clicks and long presses are also allowed. Do not treat G215/G216 examples as the only implementations. A dragging alternative also needs evaluation under 2.5.7. [See: 2.5.1-E01]

### 2.5.1-E03 — Separate author interactions and essential exceptions

- **Type / source:** [Normative — WCAG 2.2 “Pointer Gestures”](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#pointer-gestures), [Understanding — “Intent”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html#intent).
- **Summary:** Actions operating the UA or assistive technology itself are outside scope. For author implementations, check whether the multipoint or path-based gesture is essential to the function.
- **Conditions / limits:** Distinguish preserving a handwritten signature from merely obtaining consent or verifying identity. Do not classify every drawing interface as essential.

## Judgment boundaries

- Fail when actual operation confirms that an author-implemented, nonessential multipoint or path-based function has no equivalent single-pointer alternative without a path. [Evidence: 2.5.1-E01–E03]
- The tool sends no events; candidates alone establish neither conformance nor failure. Candidates or page-level manual-review nodes mean needs review; only when both are absent use not applicable within the detected scope. Zero candidates must not erase review notes for delegation, inspection failure, truncation, etc. [Our interpretation: tool mapping. Implementation: [normalizePointerGestureCheck](../../../../packages/a11y-audit/src/utils/axe-format.ts)]
- Iframe contents and listeners added after interaction can be missed. Do not generalize results to unexamined states. [Our interpretation: inspection scope. Implementation: [runPointerGestureCheck.ts](../../../../packages/a11y-audit/src/playwright/runPointerGestureCheck.ts)]

## When building

- Provide clicks or taps that perform the same function, such as carousel previous/next buttons or map zoom buttons. [Evidence: 2.5.1-E02]
- Retain the policy of avoiding path-dependent interactions outside drawing/signatures. A gesture with an alternative is not itself a WCAG failure. [Our interpretation: author's implementation policy. Supporting evidence: 2.5.1-E03]

## When checking

- **Established:** Results of checking actual interaction constraints, alternatives, and essential exceptions. [2.5.1-E01–E03]
- **Remaining:** Differences between touch and mouse, undetected and post-interaction states, and whether alternatives provide the same function.
- **Do not conclude:** An event listener alone does not establish failure. Keyboard operability alone does not establish conformance. [2.5.1-E02]
