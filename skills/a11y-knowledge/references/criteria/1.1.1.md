# 1.1.1 Non-text Content

- Components: images, image-based controls, SVG, canvas, and others. The evidence below focuses on images; it does not cover all branches for audio, video, CAPTCHA, and other content
- Evidence needed: image content, surrounding context, DOM, computed name and role, visibility, and accessibility-tree exposure
- Automated checks: axe `image-alt` / `input-image-alt` / `role-img-alt`
- Sources checked: 2026-11-01. WCAG: Recommendation of 2024-12-12; Understanding / Techniques: WCAG 2.2 published pages (updated in place). The following are summaries, not quotations.

## Rationale

### 1.1.1-E01 — Adequacy depends on purpose in context

- **Type / source:** [Understanding — “Intent”, “Examples”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-content.html#intent).
- **Summary:** The same image may need different alternatives in different uses. Images used as controls need their purpose conveyed; complex information may require more than a short name.
- **Conditions / limits:** Name presence does not establish adequacy. Inspect the image and surrounding explanations to identify what would be lost. Do not decide solely from categories such as photograph, logo, or large image.

### 1.1.1-E02 — Decoration requires both content and implementation checks

- **Type / source:** [Normative — WCAG 2.2 note on “pure decoration”](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#dfn-pure-decoration), [Technique — H67 “Description”, “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H67#tests) (Sufficient).
- **Summary:** Text can be decorative when rearranging its words would not change its purpose. H67 uses empty `alt` and absent or empty `title` for images that may be ignored.
- **Conditions / limits:** H67 presupposes that the image may be ignored. `alt=""` is not evidence that the content is decorative. Hiding informative images does not bypass the requirement.

### 1.1.1-E03 — Missing alt and missing alternatives are different

- **Type / source:** [Failure — F65 “Description”, “Tests”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F65#tests).
- **Summary:** For `img`, `area`, and `input type="image"`, check accessibility-supported alternatives through ARIA or other sources as well as `alt`. Do not rely on automatic filename announcements.
- **Conditions / limits:** Missing `alt` alone does not establish F65. Check other naming sources and treatment of images that may be ignored. A named parent alone does not establish conformance of its child image.

### 1.1.1-E04 — Name presence and name content are separate checks

- **Type / source:** ACT (Approved) [23a2a8 “Applicability”, “Expectation”, “Accessibility Requirements Mapping”](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/23a2a8/#applicability) (version: 2023-08-31), [the same sections of qt1vmo](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/qt1vmo/#applicability) (version: 2023-08-30).
- **Summary:** 23a2a8 checks naming or decorative treatment; qt1vmo checks whether a name serves an equivalent purpose. Passing either does not establish criterion-wide conformance.
- **Conditions / limits:** The former excludes programmatically hidden elements. The latter applies to visible, named `img` / `canvas` / `svg` elements, excluding cases such as an ancestor named by the author. Exclusion from a rule does not mean exclusion from WCAG. Do not assume axe implements identical checks.

### 1.1.1-E05 — Use descriptive identification for tests and sensory experiences

- **Type / source:** [Normative — WCAG 2.2 “Non-text Content,” Test / Sensory](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/#non-text-content).
- **Summary:** For tests or exercises that would be invalidated by a textual equivalent, and content primarily providing a specific sensory experience, supply at least descriptive identification.
- **Conditions / limits:** This is not permission to provide an empty alternative. Do not generalize brief identification to ordinary informational diagrams or functional images.

## Judgment lines

- Apply E05’s descriptive-identification requirements to tests and sensory experiences; otherwise, fail when the alternative demonstrably omits the image's information or control purpose. If evidence is insufficient, identify what is missing and leave it as needs review. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E01, 1.1.1-E05]
- Text, a large hero image, or a carousel is not sufficient reason to reject decorative treatment. Check content, function, and redundancy with surrounding information. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E01, 1.1.1-E02]
- Do not infer conformance or non-applicability solely from `alt=""`, `aria-hidden="true"`, or absence from the tree. Check visible information and other states in which content appears. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E02, 1.1.1-E04]
- For image-only buttons and links, check both the name describing the action and exposure of the child image. A parent's name does not automatically excuse a missing child alternative. Check the action purpose of `input type="image"` too. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E01, 1.1.1-E03]
- Also inspect non-text controls from the control side. Consolidate duplicate findings in the report and list the relevant criteria. [Our interpretation: report deduplication. Evidence: 1.1.1-E01]

## Judgment examples

- For a test image asking the user to identify a shape, provide descriptive identification of the test image rather than requiring the answer in its alternative. For an artwork primarily offered as a sensory experience, identify the work without requiring prose to reproduce the experience completely. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E05. Our application examples]
- A button containing visible “Search” text and a magnifying-glass image with no additional information does not need a duplicate image description when the image is ignored, for example through empty alt. The button name conveys the purpose and the child image is appropriately handled. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E01–E03]
- When surrounding prose fully explains a diagram’s information, brief identification and a reference to that prose suffice; do not require all details to be repeated in alt. Check for missing information when only the diagram conveys additional trends or relationships. [Evidence: 1.1.1-E01]

## When building

- Name the parent for its action and make child images without additional information ignorable. For ordinary `img` elements, provide `alt`, empty when decorative. [1.1.1-E01–E03]
- Provide the explanation needed for complex images. Judge whether the necessary information is conveyed, not merely the length of the name. [1.1.1-E01]

## When checking

- **Settled:** Fail when missing or inadequate alternatives, or inappropriate exposure of images that must be ignorable, are confirmed for the target and its context. [1.1.1-E01–E03]
- **Remaining:** After automated passes, check adequacy, decorative treatment, and content or states outside the tool's scope. [1.1.1-E04]
- **Do not assert:** Do not decide conformance or failure from missing alt alone, name presence alone, or without inspecting image content. For audio, video, CAPTCHA, and other content outside this reference's coverage, additionally check the relevant branches in WCAG itself. [1.1.1-E01–E04]
